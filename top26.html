Agents whose brokerage has an in-house lender they don't use, and who spread their loans across several lenders. Q1 2027 referral-partner list for a mortgage loan officer.
📓 Opener & pushback playbook
Your opener
"Hey [name], this is Scott — I'm a lender here in Colorado. I'm not calling to take anyone's place. Your brokerage has an in-house lender, and a lot of your buyers still end up with someone else. When a file gets weird, I want to be the name you call."
If they push back
"I already have a lender."
First, ask: "Who's your lender?"

Then: "Keep them. This isn't about me being your go-to today. It's about the deal that stalled — the one your in-house shop passed on, or the buyer who went quiet. Let me be the backup so that commission still gets paid."
"We have to use our in-house lender."
"A lot of brokerages say that, and then the buyer still picks someone else. I'm not asking you to move the easy ones. I'm asking about the file that already left the building."
"I don't have anything stuck right now."
"Then I'm the easiest call you'll get today. When one does stall — and one will — I'll give you a straight answer fast, and if it's a no I'll tell you it's a no."
"Not interested."
"No problem. Can I send you the Monday market note? No pitch, and you can tell me to stop anytime."
The line that works
"I don't just want you to send me business — but if you ever want to do some things to generate business together, I'm 100% open."
No ask, no referral request, no pitch. Use it near the end, then stop talking.

[COMPLIANCE] If they take you up on it, anything co-branded has to be fair market value with a documented, proportional cost split and cannot be tied to referrals. Route it through compliance before you agree to specifics.
Never say
No rate, APR or payment figure — not even a ballpark. Never "we'll get it closed" or "we never lose one" — say "let's see if it's doable" and "I'll tell you fast." Never trade services for referrals. If they say stop, skip them and note it.
